<h1 class="page-header">
    Importance of Pet Microchip
  </h1>
  
<p class='text'>
<table>
  <tr>
    <td style='width:685px'>
      <p class='text'>
        A microchip is a passive RFID chip, about the size of a rice grain which is implanted under a pet's skin. It contains a unique 15 digit number which a microchip scanner can identify.<br><br>

        In Singapore, there is no regulation to enforce pet owners to register the microchip number and their personal details with
        <a href='http://www.ava.gov.sg/' target='_blank'>AVA</a>,
        <a href='http://www.spca.org.sg/' target='_blank'>SPCA</a> or
        <a href='http://www.petcall.org/' target='_blank'>Petcall</a>. If someone finds a pet with microchip but the number is not registered, the owner cannot be traced. <br><br>
      </p>
    <td>
    <td>
      <img src="{{URL::to('/')}}/images/microchip.jpg" height='150px'>
      <img src="{{URL::to('/')}}/images/microchipscanner.jpg" height='150px'>
    </td>

  </tr>
</table>

Therefore, pet owners are strongly advised to register their pet's microchip number and their personal details with AVA, SPCA and Petcall. If a pet is missing or someone finds a pet, <b>microchipping AND registration</b> can highly increase the chances of reuniting the pet with its owner. In addition, pet owners can put a tag on the collar of their pets which states contact number, microchip number or address.<br><br>

<table>
  <tr>
    <td style='width:685px; vertical-align:top;'>
      <p class='text'>
        Pet owners can take <b>preventive</b> measures to ensure their pet does not go missing:<br>
        When someone opens the main door, ensure the pet does not dash out.<br>
        When someone is walking the pet, ensure he has the leash looped around his hand.<br>
        <br>

        In 2008, SPCA took in 1,162 lost dogs. Only 38% of them were claimed by their owners. Majority of them had no microchip or tag identification.
      </p>
    <td>
